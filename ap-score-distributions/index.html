    What each AP score means

    AP Exams are scored on a scale of 1 to 5. The AP score scale table gives the recommendation each score carries about how qualified a student is for college credit and placement, plus an equivalent college course grade.

    AP scoreRecommendationCollege course grade equivalent
    5Extremely well qualifiedA+ or A
    4Very well qualifiedA-, B+, or B
    3QualifiedB-, C+, or C
    2Possibly qualifiednot listed
    1No recommendationnot listed

    Composite scores get translated onto that 5-point scale using statistical processes designed so that a 3 this year reflects the same level of achievement as a 3 last year. That's why the years in the table above are worth reading side by side.

    Where these numbers come from

    Every percentage, student count and mean score here is read from a source-stamped dataset built from the published AP score distributions, then rendered in code. None of it is typed into the copy, and none of it is recalled from memory. Each year in the table links the sheet its figures came from, so you can check any row against the original.

    I transcribe each distribution sheet from the test maker's official release myself and version-stamp it with the exam and the year before it reaches a page. The calculators here refuse to show a cutoff whose vetted table hasn't loaded. Same rule applies on this page. Where a year or a single field is missing you'll see it marked missing, never a zero, and never a line drawn between two years I don't have.
